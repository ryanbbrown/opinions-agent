The test is whether a well-funded newcomer can reach parity by studying today's code, data, and system state: if it can, the knowledge converges and the moat is a countdown; if it cannot because critical dynamics were learned only through entangled real-world failures, the knowledge diverges and compounds. Customers therefore pay not just for what an incumbent already knows, but for continuing learning on their behalf, especially when verification is costly and failure is damaging. Operating successfully under stress also earns customer trust; that trust invites harder problems, which deepen operational knowledge and earn still more trust, making it part of the compounding moat. This moat survives AI because reality—not computation—sets the learning rate, but it disappears if a new technology replaces the underlying system rather than merely changing it, as AI replaced Chegg's human-mediated question-and-answer loop.

Every service business has three layers, and they are being absorbed by AI at very different speeds. Layer 1 is production work (filing the tax return, drafting the contract, generating the report, posting the invoice, sending the follow-up) — hours-for-dollars execution that is being eaten first, fastest, and most visibly. Layer 2 is pattern application: translating a known problem into an answer with a playbook the industry refined over decades (onboarding a client, handling a routine claim, reconciling a month-end close); it is next, but the pace is highly domain-specific, faster where ground truth is clean (tax, payroll, some compliance filings) and much slower where regulatory or trust constraints bind (healthcare diagnoses, legal advice, underwriting). Layer 3 is strategic direction — picking the right problem, naming what the customer actually needs, framing the engagement, deciding what to refuse — and it is not being absorbed in any meaningful way; it is getting more valuable as execution cost collapses toward the marginal cost of inference. The practical consequence: selling Layers 1 and 2 by the hour means competing with agents doing the same work for an order of magnitude less, while selling Layer 3 plus packaged Layer 1+2 outcomes positions you to be the agent.
